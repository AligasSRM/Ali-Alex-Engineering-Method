# Section 01 — Identity and Core Purpose

**Status:** 🔴 RED — structure exists; substantive review and acceptance are pending.

## Purpose
Define the project's identity, reason for existence, intended users, core value, boundaries, guiding principles, and measurable definition of success.

## Files in this section
- `VISION.md` — desired future state.
- `MISSION.md` — why the product exists and what it does.
- `CORE-PRINCIPLES.md` — principles guiding product and engineering decisions.
- `TARGET-USERS.md` — intended users and problems to solve.
- `VALUE-PROPOSITION.md` — distinct value delivered to users.
- `PRODUCT-BOUNDARIES.md` — scope and explicit exclusions.
- `ACCOUNT-IDENTITY-AND-ACCESS-SCOPE.md` — explicit discovery checklist for registration, sign-in, name/username, profile, account lifecycle, privacy, and access.
- `SUCCESS-CRITERIA.md` — measurable outcomes and evidence.
- `ASSUMPTIONS-AND-RISKS.md` — unverified assumptions and initial risks.
- `ACCEPTANCE-CRITERIA.md` — conditions required to complete this section.
- `DECISIONS.md` — approved decisions and rationale.

## Traceability and cross-document consistency
Use `02-planning-before-implementation/REQUIREMENT-TRACEABILITY-MATRIX.md` as the canonical location for requirement IDs and links from approved identity/scope decisions to design, implementation, tests, and release disposition. Keep product facts, assumptions, and hypotheses explicitly distinguished. Before acceptance, compare vision, mission, target users, value proposition, product boundaries, account/identity scope, success criteria, assumptions/risks, and decisions for contradictions.
- `STATUS.md` — current status, gaps, evidence, and next action.

## Dependencies
This section informs planning, architecture, scope, security, testing, and release decisions across the methodology. Account and identity capabilities must be explicitly accepted, deferred, or marked not applicable rather than silently omitted.

## Completion rule
The section cannot be marked GREEN until its documents are reviewed, key assumptions are explicitly identified, boundaries are agreed, and acceptance criteria are supported by recorded evidence. Creating these files alone does not satisfy completion.
